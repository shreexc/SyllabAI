# 1. Document Purpose

This document identifies, categorizes, analyzes, and manages the stakeholders who may affect, be affected by, contribute to, approve, use, operate, support, regulate, or depend on SyllabAI.

The purpose of the Stakeholder Register is to ensure that the project does not focus only on direct users.

A complete stakeholder analysis considers:

```text
People
   +
Teams
   +
Organizations
   +
Customers
   +
Partners
   +
Service Providers
   +
Decision Makers
   +
Regulators
   +
System Operators
   +
Indirectly Affected Parties
```

Stakeholder identification should continue throughout the project because new stakeholders may emerge as the product, architecture, business model, and deployment environment become clearer.

---

# 2. Stakeholder Management Principle

A stakeholder is any person, group, organization, or external entity that:

- Uses SyllabAI.
- Provides content to SyllabAI.
- Operates SyllabAI.
- Makes decisions about SyllabAI.
- Funds SyllabAI.
- Approves SyllabAI.
- Builds SyllabAI.
- Supports SyllabAI.
- Depends on SyllabAI.
- Provides services required by SyllabAI.
- Is affected by SyllabAI.
- Can influence project success or failure.
- Has legitimate concerns about the product.

# 6. Detailed Stakeholder Profiles

# 6.1 Student

## Stakeholder ID

`ST-001`

## Role

Primary user and learner.

## Description

Students are the primary consumers of educational content and learning functionality within SyllabAI.

They may use the platform to:

- Access subjects.
- Read notes.
- View PDFs.
- Watch educational resources.
- Complete assignments.
- Attempt quizzes.
- Track progress.
- Ask educational questions.
- Use AI assistance.
- Review performance.

## Interest

**High**

Students directly depend on the quality, usability, accessibility, and usefulness of the platform.

## Influence

**High**

Student adoption and retention can strongly affect product success.

## Impact

**High**

Students are directly affected by:

- Platform usability.
- Content availability.
- AI accuracy.
- Performance.
- Privacy.
- Reliability.

## Expectations

Students may expect:

- Easy navigation.
- Fast access.
- Reliable content.
- Useful search.
- Clear progress information.
- Useful AI assistance.
- Mobile-friendly access.
- Secure accounts.

## Concerns

- Poor usability.
- Incorrect AI answers.
- Privacy.
- Data loss.
- Slow performance.
- Missing resources.
- Difficult navigation.

## Engagement Strategy

**Manage Closely**

Students should participate in:

- User research.
- Usability testing.
- MVP validation.
- Feedback collection.
- Product experiments.

# 10. Engagement Strategy

## 10.1 Manage Closely

These stakeholders require active participation and regular communication.

Examples:

- Students during validation.
- Teachers during workflow design.
- Product Owner.
- Sponsor.
- Technical Lead.
- Platform Administrator.
- Development Leads.
- AI/ML Lead.
- DevOps Lead.
- Institutional decision makers.

### Engagement Methods

- Workshops.
- Interviews.
- Reviews.
- Demonstrations.
- Planning meetings.
- Requirement reviews.
- Decision meetings.

---

# 10.2 Keep Satisfied

Stakeholders with high influence but less frequent operational involvement.

Examples:

- Regulatory authorities.
- Legal advisors.
- Major infrastructure providers.
- Education authorities.

### Engagement Methods

- Formal updates.
- Compliance reviews.
- Documentation.
- Approval requests.
- Escalation when required.

---

# 10.3 Keep Informed

Stakeholders who should receive appropriate project information.

Examples:

- Parents/guardians.
- Wider education community.
- Certain support groups.

### Engagement Methods

- Announcements.
- Documentation.
- Periodic updates.
- Feedback channels.

---

# 10.4 Monitor

Stakeholders with relatively low influence and low immediate interest.

Examples:

- General public.
- Wider external communities.

Monitoring should continue because stakeholder influence can change over time.

# 15. Stakeholder Needs vs Product Requirements

Stakeholder needs should eventually become documented requirements.

Example:

```text
Stakeholder
     ↓
Need
     ↓
Requirement
     ↓
Feature
     ↓
Acceptance Criteria
     ↓
Test Case
```

Example:

```text
Student
  ↓
Needs organized resources
  ↓
System shall organize resources by subject
  ↓
Subject Resource Module
  ↓
Acceptance Criteria
  ↓
QA Test
```

This traceability should be maintained throughout the SDLC.

# 16. Stakeholder Conflict Management

Different stakeholders may have conflicting priorities.

Examples:

### Student vs Teacher

Students may want:

- More resources.
- More flexibility.
- Easier access.

Teachers may want:

- More control.
- Less administrative work.
- Content protection.

---

### Product vs Engineering

Product may request:

- Faster delivery.
- More features.

Engineering may require:

- More design time.
- Refactoring.
- Security work.
- Testing.

---

### User Convenience vs Security

Users may prefer:

- Fewer login steps.

Security requirements may require:

- Strong authentication.
- Verification.
- Access controls.

---

### AI Capability vs Reliability

Users may want:

- More AI features.

Engineering/QA may require:

- Evaluation.
- Safety controls.
- Cost controls.
- Reliability testing.

---

# 17. Stakeholder Conflict Resolution Principles

When conflicts occur, decisions should consider:

1. User value.
2. Product vision.
3. Business objectives.
4. Security.
5. Legal requirements.
6. Technical feasibility.
7. Project constraints.
8. Long-term maintainability.
9. Evidence from user research.
10. Measurable outcomes.

Important decisions should be documented rather than made informally.

# 22. Stakeholder Change Management

Stakeholders may change throughout the project.

Examples:

- New institution joins.
- New teacher group becomes a target.
- Product introduces paid subscriptions.
- Students become minors in a new market.
- New AI provider is introduced.
- Regulatory requirements change.
- New business partners are added.

When a significant stakeholder change occurs:

1. Identify the stakeholder.
2. Analyze interest.
3. Analyze influence.
4. Identify expectations.
5. Identify risks.
6. Define engagement strategy.
7. Update this register.
8. Update affected requirements.

# 30. Stakeholder Analysis Questions

For every important stakeholder, the team should be able to answer:

### Who are they?

What person, group, or organization are we dealing with?

### What do they want?

What outcome do they expect?

### What do they need?

What must the system provide for them?

### What can they influence?

Which decisions can they affect?

### What can affect them?

Which system decisions can create benefits or risks for them?

### What are their concerns?

What could cause resistance or dissatisfaction?

### What information do they need?

What should the project communicate to them?

### How should we engage them?

What communication and participation model is appropriate?

### Who owns the relationship?

Which team member is responsible for engagement?

# 32. Stakeholder Management Success Criteria

Stakeholder management will be considered effective when:

- Major stakeholders have been identified.
- Stakeholder roles are documented.
- Stakeholder expectations are understood.
- Stakeholder responsibilities are clear.
- Communication responsibilities are assigned.
- Important feedback is captured.
- Conflicts are documented and managed.
- Stakeholder-related risks are tracked.
- Requirements can be traced to stakeholder needs.
- Stakeholder changes are reflected in project documentation.
